🎛️ Interactive Multiplier Explorer

Drag the sliders to see how three approximate multiplication rules (column truncation, DRUM, RoBA) change the product of two unsigned 8-bit integers. This page computes numbers only; it does not model area, delay, power or energy.

Guided tasks: what each control does (static text)

Before you start: binary place values, and the four steps of multiplication (generate, arrange by weight, compress, add) from the “Start here” part of the Approximate Multipliers page. Small example: 3 × 5 is 0011 × 0101; the 1-bits of B give the rows 0011 (3) and 1100 (12), and 3 + 12 = 15.

Controls and readings

  • Operand A and Operand B: whole numbers from 1 to 255; the label shows the value and its 8-bit binary form. Drag a slider, or click it and use the arrow keys (one step per press).
  • Approximation Architecture: the exact reference, column truncation (partial-product bits with i + j < 4 are never generated), unsigned RoBA (operands rounded to powers of two) or DRUM-4 (a 4-bit window with its lowest bit forced to 1). The grey box under the menu explains the chosen rule for the current operands.
  • Exact Answer (A × B) and Approximate Output: the decimal value, with its 16-bit binary pattern below.
  • Relative Error Distance (RED): |approximate − exact| ÷ exact, for this one operand pair.
  • Hardware Cost: always “Not modelled”; this lab computes numbers only.
  • Exhaustive statistics (bottom box): all 65,025 operand pairs for the chosen rule. It does not change when you move the sliders.

Task 1. Predict the four outputs for 3 × 5. Then set A = 3 and B = 5 and choose each option in the menu. Why does column truncation give 0?

Task 2. With RoBA still on 3 × 5, read Ar and Br in the grey box and check the output by hand: Ar·B + Br·A − Ar·Br.

Task 3. Set A = 25, B = 3 and choose DRUM-4. Read the A segment and its shift in the grey box. What number does the segment stand for after the shift?
